Manideep Pasupula

PhD Candidate, Physics of Fluids, University of Twente

I study how the daily cycle of the atmospheric boundary layer shapes the way large wind farms extract energy, block the incoming flow and trigger atmospheric gravity waves, using high-fidelity large-eddy simulation.

I am a PhD candidate in the Physics of Fluids group at the University of Twente in Enschede, the Netherlands, working in the wind-energy research team of Prof. Richard Stevens. My doctoral project, Diurnal boundary-layer forcing, blockage, and wind-farm performance, examines how day–night changes in surface heating alter the flow around and inside large wind farms.

I trained as an aerospace engineer at the Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur (dual degree, B.Tech. Honours and M.Tech., 2018–2023), where my master's thesis dealt with the aerodynamic design of a small-size transonic contra-rotating fan stage. I then worked as a Mechanical Design Engineer at Honeywell Technology Solutions in the fan and compressor team, on aerodynamics, design optimisation and CFD. That background in turbomachinery and compressible flows now feeds into my work on atmospheric turbulence and wind-farm flow physics.

    research

    Themes I work on, from the blade row to the planetary boundary layer.

    Diurnal boundary-layer forcing

    How the daily cycle of surface heat flux, from convective afternoons to stable nights, changes the inflow, wake recovery and power output of large wind farms.

    Wind-farm blockage

    The slowdown of the incoming wind upstream of a farm, and why its magnitude depends so strongly on atmospheric stability and the capping inversion.

    Atmospheric gravity waves

    Wind farms can displace the boundary-layer top and radiate gravity waves into the free atmosphere, feeding back on the pressure field the turbines experience.

    Large-eddy simulation

    High-resolution LES of turbulent atmospheric flow with turbine models, run on high-performance computing systems to resolve farm-scale dynamics.

    Turbomachinery aerodynamics

    Aerodynamic design and CFD of fans and compressors, from a transonic contra-rotating fan stage at IIT Kharagpur to industrial fan and compressor design at Honeywell.

    Internship · Honeywell · 2022

    Tubercles on the NASA SDT fan

    CFD study of humpback-whale-inspired leading-edge tubercles on the NASA SDT fan rotor as passive flow control to improve stall behaviour.

    Designed, fabricated and tested propellers with tubercles and spheres on the leading edge, alongside ANSYS Fluent simulations, with Prof. Sandeep Saha.
